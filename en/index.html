Capabilities

What it does

A single point of contact for the small errands of every day.

📅

Calendar & reminders

Creates Google Calendar events and sends reminders that keep nudging until you confirm them.

✉️

Messages & email

Drafts messages and emails and only sends them after an explicit confirmation.

🔎

Web research

Searches multiple sources, opens the pages and reports results with their original links.

📄

Files & documents

Reads PDFs, documents and images, extracts the text and converts files between formats.

⏱️

Scheduled tasks

Runs jobs at set times and keeps an eye on pages and prices, warning you when they change.

🎨

Local images

Generates images right on the server, without going through external services.

The method

How it works

Three steps, always the same.

A request

You write on Telegram the way you'd write to a person, with no commands to remember.

The action

The assistant checks the real state of things, acts with the tools it has, and verifies the result.

The report

It replies with what it did, what it verified, and what's still open.

Under the hood

How it's built

In short, without too much jargon.

💬

Telegram as the interface

No app to install: you talk to the assistant from the chat, on phone or computer.

🖥️

Private server

It runs on the owner's always-on machine, which carries out requests and keeps the history.

🧠

AI models

It uses AI models, some local on the server and some in the cloud, depending on the task.

🧩

Sub-agents

For bigger jobs it splits the task into parts and hands them to specialised helpers.

Doubts

Frequently asked

The things people usually ask first.

Can I use it too?

No: SkyNet is a personal project, designed and configured for a single owner. It's not a public service and there's no sign-up.

Where does it run?

On an always-on private server, reachable only by the owner through the Telegram chat. There's no website or app for anyone else to control it.

Who sees my data?

Only the owner. Data stays on their server; to process a request its content may be sent to an AI model solely to get the answer. Details are in the privacy notice.

How does it avoid sending things by mistake?

It always prepares a draft and waits for an explicit go-ahead before sending messages, emails, or running actions that delete or change something.

How is it different from a voice assistant?

It doesn't just answer: it checks the real state of things, acts with the tools it has, then verifies it actually got the result, telling you what it did.
